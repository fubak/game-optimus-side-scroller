Round history

Round 5 — Second biome — The Foundry

2026-08-16
  • The Foundry: enclosed, cold, lit entirely from inside the frame by molten channels below and magenta signage in the middle distance.
  • Structure changed as well as palette. Manufactured silhouettes generated from stacked structural bays rather than noise, since noise produces organic profiles however it is tuned. Light comes from below so the value ramp inverts. The room is a descent rather than a traverse.
  • Biome switching costs no texture work: every biome's art is baked into one atlas at load, so a switch is a change of layer list, atmosphere, room, and light rig.
  • Enemy placement derived from the room's own platforms rather than a hardcoded table.
  • Rim light is now modulated by albedo. It was being added independently, lifting every dark surface by the full rim colour.
  • Fill light is per-biome. The broad warm fill belongs to an open exterior; carried into an interior it erased the pools of practical light the space is built around.
  • The gate now runs both biomes end to end.

Biggest remaining gap: Bosses and progression. There is now a second biome and combat that reads correctly, but no boss encounter, no ranged attack or parry, no room-to-room progression, no save, and no menus - the game is two disconnected demo rooms rather than a structure. Biomes 3 and 4 are also still unbuilt. Next: the first boss, then the room graph and title/pause shell.

Round 4 — Playable game — particles, combat, enemies, audio, HUD, respawn

2026-08-16
  • GPU-instanced particle system: several thousand motes in two draw calls, with ambient dust on curl noise, ballistic debris, buoyant smoke, and velocity-oriented sparks. Emitters wired to landings, dashes, running, and every impact.
  • Combat: three-hit melee combo with buffered cancels, hitboxes as timed activations de-duplicated per target, and centralised resolution of hitstop, knockback, contact flash, screen shake, and sparks so they cannot drift apart.
  • Sentry drone enemy built from a deliberately different visual vocabulary - angular chassis, amber optic - so it is distinguishable from the player at a glance. Every state change telegraphs before it matters.
  • Ribbon trails generated from the hand's real path rather than a canned crescent, so arcs match whatever the animation is doing.
  • Fully procedural audio: synthesised SFX from a three-ingredient house palette, a generative wind bed, and layered adaptive music where combat brings in pad and pulse layers rather than swapping tracks.
  • Holographic HUD with a spring-driven integrity gauge and a slow ghost bar showing damage just taken.
  • Death and respawn with ground-verified checkpoints and a full-screen wipe.
  • Terrain now tiles instead of stretching, which restores surface detail across long platforms.

Biggest remaining gap: Content breadth and character animation detail. Blind review now confirms hitstop, hit flash, knockback, foot planting, and jump arcs all read correctly, and rates combat impact 4/10 and animation fluidity 3/10 - the weakness is no longer that effects are missing but that the animation set is thin. There is one biome, one enemy type, no boss, no ranged attack, no parry, no progression, and no menus. Next: a second enemy, the first boss, and richer transition animations (turnarounds, landing rolls, attack recovery blends).

Round 3 — Atmosphere — god rays that are actually in frame

2026-08-16
  • God rays were anchored to a fixed world position, which is off-screen at every camera position in the level. A radial effect fades to nothing once its source leaves the frame, so the shafts were never visible anywhere. The source now follows the camera along the sun's direction, as an infinitely distant light should.
  • Terrain now occludes the sky, so shafts have something to break over. Previously only props, the player, and the foreground band wrote to the occluder mask, leaving the largest silhouette in the scene absent from it.
  • Added a fully-composited debug view, turning a tuning iteration from a ~60 second clip recording into an ~8 second preview.
  • Shaft energy tuned down after an overcorrection washed the entire frame into a milky haze: the radial accumulation brightens all open sky, not only the shafted regions, so its exposure has to stay modest.

Biggest remaining gap: Particle density and bloom energy. Bloom measures 0.001-0.010 against a 0.015-0.06 target, and the dust field is a few hundred motes drawn as individual quads where the reference bar carries thousands. There are no wind gusts, no ground-level drift, no debris on landing, and no impact effects at all. The near/far contrast ratio also regressed to 1.08 on the traversal framing, below the 1.8 floor, because the foreground band and the shafts both lift the near band. Next round: a GPU-instanced particle system, then combat VFX.

Round 2 — Grounding — contact occlusion and a working shadow term

2026-08-16
  • Rewrote shadow accumulation as a soft maximum along the ray instead of a mean. Averaging produced about 4% darkening and was invisible: a character is roughly 0.03 of the screen wide while the sun ray spanned 0.55, so at most one sample in sixteen could land on it, and dividing that single hit by the sample count erased it.
  • Shadow *reception* now falls off with parallax depth. Every occluder lives in the playfield plane, so without it a crate cast a long dark band across a mesa fifty metres behind it.
  • Added a dedicated contact-occlusion buffer. WebGL2 has no per-attachment blend state, so an earlier multiply blend aimed at the albedo also scaled the normal, material, and depth attachments — flipping normals inside the affected rectangle and rendering a soft pool as a hard black box.
  • Contact pools shrink and fade with height above the ground, so a jump reads as leaving the surface rather than merely translating upward.

Biggest remaining gap: Bloom and volumetrics carry almost no energy. God rays render but are effectively invisible, and bloom energy measures around 0.001 against a 0.015-0.06 target on gameplay framings, so there is no atmospheric light in the frame. Next to the reference bar the image reads as correctly lit but inert: nothing glows, nothing scatters, and the air is empty. Particle density is also far below the bar - a few hundred dust motes where the reference has thousands, with no wind gusts, no ground-level drift, and no impact debris.

Round 1 — Playable — physics, controller, level, and a lighting rebalance

2026-08-16
  • Swept-AABB physics and a full player controller: coyote time, jump buffering, variable jump height, apex hang, corner correction, eight-way dash with i-frames, wall slide and wall jump.
  • First real room, sized against the measured jump envelope (3.83 m peak, 10.15 m range) so no jump needs frame-perfect input.
  • An autopilot that navigates by probing ground heights ahead, replacing brittle hand-timed input tapes. It also acts as a smoke test and now verifies the room end to end.
  • Fixed four simulation bugs invisible in stills: a floating-point sweep rejection that dropped the character through the world, grounded-state flicker from the collision skin gap, a phantom button press every frame from tape reinstallation, and lookahead that could not see step-ups.
  • Lighting rebalance driven by the metrics: ambient lowered so the key light does the work, sky authored below the bloom threshold so only the sun and emissives glow, shell albedo lowered from near-white which had been saturating into a featureless silhouette.
  • Foreground framing re-anchored as a fraction of view height, and foreground parallax fixed so it scrolls faster than the playfield.
  • New crate art: the props previously reused the bright shell panel and read unmistakably as wall-mounted TV screens.
  • Solved the foot attachment offset from the geometry, correcting a 4.3 cm hover.

Biggest remaining gap: Cast shadows and bloom energy. Shadow casters are wired up and the occluder mask renders correctly, but no shadow is actually visible in any frame — the character does not appear anchored to the ground. Bloom energy measures 0.001 against a 0.015-0.06 target on both gameplay scenarios, so there is no god-ray or glow presence, and cyan presence is 10x below target. These three together are why the image still reads flat next to the reference bar.

Round 0 — Foundation — engine, renderer, capture harness

2026-08-16
  • Hand-written WebGL2 deferred renderer: 4-attachment MRT G-buffer, normal-mapped lighting with raymarched soft shadows, god rays, height fog, dual-Kawase bloom, ACES tonemap, LUT grade, grain.
  • Fixed 120 Hz simulation with interpolated variable-rate rendering; hitstop and time-dilation stacks; split sim/fx RNG streams.
  • Analytically-solved damped springs and cubic Hermite curves, both verified frame-rate independent from 30 to 240 Hz.
  • Deterministic capture harness on a virtual clock: records true 60 fps footage from a GPU-less machine, and is byte-reproducible for a given seed.
  • Procedural art pipeline: surfaces authored as height fields, so normal maps, ambient occlusion, and material channels are derived rather than hand-painted.
  • Optimus rig: 25 bones, proportions solved as a chain to exactly 1.73 m, inertialized transitions, spring-driven cable sway, impact compression, foot IK.

Biggest remaining gap: Not a game yet — no physics, no player input, no level.
